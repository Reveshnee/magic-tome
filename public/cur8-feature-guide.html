The Breathwork widget gives you a visual, guided breathing exercise you can start in seconds — directly inside your haven. It regulates your nervous system before a study or work session, helping you move from scattered to settled.

How it works
1

Open the Breathwork widget from the haven toolbar or The Pond on the home screen.

2

Choose a pattern — Box, 4-7-8, or Deep. The animated orb expands and contracts to guide each breath.

3

Follow along for 2–5 minutes. Your body settles — then move into focused work.

Best used for Before deep work, after difficult calls, or any time you feel overwhelmed and need to reset quickly.

The Focus Timer is a Pomodoro-style work timer that keeps you anchored to a single task. It counts down your session, tracks how many rounds you've completed, and signals when to rest — so your brain stays sharp across a long work day.

How it works
1

Tap the Focus orb in The Pond or open it from your haven toolbar.

2

Set your session length (default 25 min) and target number of rounds. Start the timer.

3

Work until the bell. Take your break. Completed sessions are shown as gold dots so you can see your momentum building.

Best used for Studying, writing, client work — any task that needs uninterrupted chunks of concentrated attention.

Reflections is your space to process and make meaning from what you've been saving. Each haven has its own reflection thread, so insights from your wellness reading stay separate from your coaching study or work research. Nothing gets lost, and your thinking compounds over time.

How it works
1

Tap Reflect in any haven toolbar (or the floating Reflect button on the home screen).

2

Read the AI-generated prompt, or skip it and write freely. You can also attach voice notes, images, or documents to any entry.

3

Save. Your reflection is stored to that haven's thread — scroll back any time to revisit your thinking, or share it directly via email or WhatsApp.

Best used for Processing lectures and books you've saved, journalling insights after coaching sessions, or capturing "aha" moments before they disappear.
